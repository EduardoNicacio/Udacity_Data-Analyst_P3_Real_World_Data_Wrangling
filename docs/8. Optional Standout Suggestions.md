# Optional Standout Suggestions

If you want to make your project stand out, try implementing some of the suggestions below. These are optional, and skipping them will not affect the standard review of your project. However, these additions can enhance your project by showcasing more advanced data wrangling, analysis, and presentation skills.

## Advanced Data Collection and Storage

- **Utilize a Relational/Non-Relational Database**: Instead of using only local storage, consider storing and updating your data in a relational (e.g., MySQL, PostgreSQL) or non-relational (e.g., MongoDB) database. This approach demonstrates strong data management skills and can provide valuable experience with more complex data storage solutions.
- **Incorporate Three or More Data Sources**: Select additional data sources to enrich your analysis. Ensure that these datasets are related and add meaningful insights to your original research question. Carefully document each source, the method of data gathering, and how it complements the other datasets in your project.

## Enhanced Data Cleaning and Exploration

- **Go Beyond Basic Cleaning**: Tackle more advanced cleaning methods, such as handling outliers or applying normalization or scaling techniques if relevant to your data. Document these steps clearly, explaining how they improve the dataset for analysis.
- **Feature Engineering**: Create new features that may reveal additional insights relevant to your research question. For example, if you’re working with time-series data, you could engineer features like moving averages or seasonal indicators. Explain the logic behind each new feature and how it enhances your analysis.

## In-Depth Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

- **Create Additional Visualizations**: Include more than two visualizations that address various aspects of your research question. Aim for diversity in your visuals (e.g., line plots, histograms, scatter plots) to highlight different relationships or patterns in your data.
- **Use Advanced Visualizations**: Go beyond basic plotting techniques by experimenting with advanced visualizations, such as heatmaps, pair plots, or interactive plots (e.g., Plotly). These visuals can provide a deeper understanding of complex relationships in the data.

## Advanced Analysis and Insights

- **Answer Multiple Research Questions**: Identify additional, related research questions and use your cleaned data to answer them. This can provide a more comprehensive analysis and demonstrate the versatility of your dataset.
- **Apply Dimensionality Reduction**: If your dataset has a large number of variables, consider using techniques like Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to reduce dimensionality while retaining meaningful information. Document your approach and discuss how dimensionality reduction impacts the results.

## Critical Self-Assessment and Reflection

- **Deepen Your Reflection**: In your reflection section, assess your data wrangling process in more depth. Consider alternative methods you could have used, and outline specific actions you would take to address limitations in time, data, or resources.
